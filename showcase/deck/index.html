An hour at the tail is not an hour at the head.

0.145m³/s at outlet 1

3.5% of the flow already lost to seepage

0.106m³/s at outlet 8

29.4% of the flow lost to seepage

Same hour of turn, about three-quarters of the water: 0.106 ÷ 0.145 ≈ 0.73.

Modelled  Q(x) = Q₀·e^(−kx), Q₀ = 0.15 m³/s, k = 0.00012 per metre, from the seed scenario. Model output, not gauge readings.

Cue: Read the two big numbers, then the ratio. Pause after the ratio. — Here is what the register misses. Using the scenario's seepage model, the flow at outlet one is 0.145 cubic metres per second, and 3.5 percent has already been lost. By outlet eight it is 0.106, and 29.4 percent has been lost. Divide the second by the first, 0.106 over 0.145, and you get about 0.73. The same hour of turn, about three-quarters of the water. These are modelled values from the seed scenario, not gauge readings.

Give each farm the volume it needs, then solve for time.

Ti = Vi ÷ Q(xi)
Ti
How long farm i holds the outlet.
Vi
The volume farm i needs this week, from the crop-water model.
Q(xi)
The flow that actually reaches its outlet after seepage. The tail's turn runs longer.

Computed in packages/core (roster, hydraulics, crop need, ledger). No water arithmetic exists anywhere else in the codebase.

Cue: Point at the equation, then at each term as you name it. — That is the idea in one line. T sub i equals V sub i divided by Q of x sub i. V is the volume farm i needs this week, from the crop-water model. Q of x is the flow that actually reaches its outlet after seepage. Time is the answer, not the input. And this arithmetic lives in one package. There is no water arithmetic anywhere else in the codebase.
