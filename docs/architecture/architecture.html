System Overview · 60-Second Summary

How Jadal Works

Jadal shares canal water by the volume each farm actually needs and receives, not by hours.

Who It Helps

Tail-End Farmer

Gets usable water delivered at the field gate; canal physics calculates longer turn times to offset conveyance seepage and travel lag.

Section 1.0 · Core Principle

Jadal

Agentic, physics-based canal water allocation for warabandi irrigation.

Rotational canal irrigation (*warabandi*) allocates water by calendar time proportional to landholding, operating under the flawed assumption that an allotted hour yields identical water volume anywhere along the canal. In physical reality, non-linear conveyance seepage ($e^{-kx}$), dynamic wetting-front travel lag ($x/v$), and upstream turn overruns attenuate the delivered discharge toward the tail reach. Because the official canal register records only elapsed clock hours, systemic tail-end deficits remain completely invisible to administrators. Jadal replaces this rigid hours roster with an agentic, volume-based accounting framework: irrigation demand is calculated dynamically from FAO-56 crop phenology, physical flow is computed through hydraulic models, entitlements are managed via a double-entry volume ledger, and multi-channel Telugu voice dialogues provide guaranteed delivery tracking.

LLMs Propose

Frontier models handle multi-turn Telugu dialogue, contextual explanations, and trade-off negotiation without ever doing water arithmetic.

Deterministic Core Computes

Pure, unit-tested TypeScript engines calculate FAO-56 evapotranspiration, Manning channel hydraulics, and double-entry mass conservation.

Coordinator Approves

Every seasonal entitlement, weekly roster release, and emergency buffer withdrawal requires human-in-the-loop sign-off before execution.

Section 2.0 · Agronomic & Institutional Context

Problem & Stakeholder Breakdown

Canal irrigation networks across Andhra Pradesh (such as the Krishna Western Delta and Nagarjuna Sagar Left Canal) govern water distribution via the historical warabandi rotational roster. Under this system, each farmer is allotted a fixed weekly time window based purely on acreage. Below is the multi-stakeholder diagnostic submitted to IEEE-CIS illustrating why this system breaks down in practice:

Stakeholder Field Testimony Underlying Institutional Failure
Upstream Farmer
Head Reach		 "My crop needs water today." Fixed calendar turns ignore physiological crop growth stages; there is no formal mechanism to request or advance water before wilting occurs.
Downstream Farmer
Tail End		 "You extended your turn; flow was weak." Head-reach overruns and unmodeled conveyance seepage deplete the discharge rate reaching the lateral tail.
Canal Coordinator
WUA Official		 "Everyone received their allotted hours." The official government register logs elapsed time rather than delivered cubic metres, rendering physical shortfall invisible.
Downstream Farmer
Tail End		 "Hours are not usable water." An identical time window delivers significantly less volume the farther a sluice gate is located from the headworks.
Small Farmer
Marginal Holder		 "I did not see the schedule change." Schedule modifications are broadcast over a single smartphone chat group with zero delivery verification or feature-phone outreach.
The Root Cause

Allocation and records are in hours, but crops need usable water delivered at the field gate, at the right growth stage.

Ten Structural Failures of Time-Based Warabandi

  • 1. Hours are not water: Seepage ($e^{-kx}$) and travel lag mean one hour at a tail outlet delivers up to 55% less water than at the head (§1.0).
  • 2. Allocation ignores crop needs: Sowing dates, soil texture, dynamic rooting depth, and multi-crop holdings are omitted from scheduling (§5.1).
  • 3. No shared starting agreement: Seasonal entitlements live in private paper notebooks, verbal memory, or informal coordinator logs (§4.0).
  • 4. Release timing is unpredictable: Main canal water often arrives in evening or night (18:00–06:00); unwarned farmers miss turns or face hazardous night irrigation (§9.2).
  • 5. Rain is ignored: Fixed calendar rosters flush canal water immediately after storms; rainfall conservation is not banked for dry spells (§5.1).
  • 6. Urgency is settled by force: In the absence of an emergency request protocol, farmers breach canal bunds or physically guard sluice gates (§7.0).
  • 7. Unused water is wasted: Post-harvest quota remainders and skipped turns are lost rather than pooled in a communal reserve (§8.0).
  • 8. Announcements exclude people: Unidirectional WhatsApp messages fail to reach non-smartphone farmers and provide zero delivery receipts (§9.1).
  • 9. Records cannot settle disputes: The hours register cannot prove volumetric shortfalls or quantify upstream overrun impact (§5.3).
  • 10. The coordinator cannot do the maths by hand: Solving multi-variable non-linear hydraulics, soil water balances, and release windows manually is impossible (§5.0).
Section 3.0 · Architectural Layering

Three-Layer Architecture: S₀, System 1, and System 2

Water allocation in an arid or semi-arid canal command is a strict zero-sum conservation problem governed by physical mass balance and travel lags. Large Language Models (LLMs) hallucinate arithmetic when performing numerical optimization. Conversely, static rule engines cannot handle colloquial rural Telugu dialects or negotiate empathetic trade-offs. Jadal resolves this tension through strict architectural stratification:

Section 4.0 · Operational Flow

Seasonal Lifecycle: From Registration to Close & Audit

Managing an irrigation season under Jadal progresses through seven structured stages. Rather than a static, unalterable schedule, Jadal operates as an adaptive state machine driven by weather forecasts, crop stage advancements, emergency requests, and farmer acknowledgements:

Step-by-Step Lifecycle Explanation:

  1. Registration: Before water release, farmers register plot areas ($A$ in ha), soil texture (such as Vertisol clay, clay loam, or sandy loam), crop varieties, sowing dates, and canal outlet chainage ($x$ in metres) via the local portal, voice intake, or WhatsApp.
  2. AI-Suggested Entitlement: The Need Agent executes the FAO-56 crop-need engine across historical and forecast $ET_0$ and rainfall to calculate weekly gross cubic metres per plot, bounded by root-zone soil water storage limits ($[V_{\min}, V_{\max}]$, based on RAW and TAW).
  3. Coordinator Review: The WUA coordinator reviews the proposed seasonal quotas against total reservoir canal supply ($180,000\text{ m}^3$ illustrative demo value). The coordinator authorizes the entitlement ledger, writing initial balances to the volume ledger and seeding unallocated water to the common buffer.
  4. Release-Window Roster: When the Irrigation Department notifies a release window (such as 72 hours at head discharge $Q_0 = 0.15\text{ m}^3/\text{s}$), the Scheduler Agent uses canal hydraulics (discharge $Q_i$ and travel lag $x_i / v$) to pack turns from head to tail ($T_i = V_i / Q_i + \text{lag}_i$), ensuring downstream gates open only after the wetting front arrives. The coordinator approves the roster.
  5. Outreach & Mandatory Acknowledgement: The Caller Agent contacts farmers in Telugu via voice and WhatsApp. Evening or night releases (18:00 to 06:00) trigger a WhatsApp message and a warning call 1 hour before flow arrives. Any roster change requires an affirmative acknowledgement (`ACK_RECEIVED`) before being marked active.
  6. In-Season Adaptive Loop:
    • Turn Delivered: Sluice gate opens and closes; delivered volume and conveyance losses are logged atomically to the volume ledger (`farmer:quota` $\rightarrow$ `farmer:delivered` + `losses:conveyance`), advancing rotation to the next turn.
    • Rain Re-plan: If Open-Meteo detects rainfall $\ge 15\text{ mm}$ that satisfies root-zone need, active turns are cancelled or deferred, and saved water transfers to the common buffer (`farmer:quota` $\rightarrow$ `buffer`).
    • Urgent Request: If a farmer faces crop failure, a distress call triggers fast System 1 triage (Jev/Laya score $\ge 4/5$). The Request Assessor evaluates risk, and upon coordinator approval, volume is borrowed from future quotas (`future_quota` $\rightarrow$ `active_turn`) to schedule an immediate turn.
    • Buffer Credit / Claim: Farmers who do not need water surrender turns to the communal buffer. Deficit farmers submit public buffer requests (capped at 25% of weekly quota) with transparent coordinator approval (`buffer` $\rightarrow$ `farmer:turn`).
    • Harvest Exit: When an early crop matures, the farmer declares harvest and donates remaining seasonal quota to the community buffer pool (`remaining_quota` $\rightarrow$ `buffer`). If other plots remain active, rotation continues; once all crops finish, execution exits to season close.
  7. Season Close & Audit: At season termination, the Auditor Agent evaluates the double-entry ledger invariant across all transactions ($\text{season\_supply} \equiv \sum \text{quotas} + \text{buffer} + \sum \text{delivered} + \text{losses}$) and computes the Gini coefficient to prove equitable distribution between head and tail reaches (demonstrating tail reach need met rising from $\sim 45\%$ under equal hours to $>90\%$ under equal water).
Section 5.0 · Physics & Agronomic Engine

Deterministic Core (S₀): Equations & Physical Constraints

The deterministic core (S₀) is a pure, unit-tested TypeScript library (packages/core) running inside Cloudflare Workers. It contains zero LLM dependencies and never performs probabilistic guessing. The core comprises four sub-blocks that govern agronomic water balance, open-channel fluid mechanics, schedule optimization, and financial-grade volumetric accounting.

Orchestrator Review & Agronomic Lineage (FAO-56 2025 Revision)

All crop water calculations strictly adhere to the FAO-56 2025 revision (Pereira et al., 2025; ISBN 978-92-5-140060-9) and the verification review in docs/research/fao56-model.md:
1. Crop Parameter Source: Crop parameters load from verified tables in docs/research/fao56-crop-tables.md and docs/research/fao56-book-reference.md. Feature code never uses hardcoded memory values.
2. Thermal Time vs Calendar Days: Static stage-length tables (old 1998 Table 11) are replaced by thermal time (Growing Degree Days, GDD) from Tables 6.10–6.12. The engine dynamically converts accumulated GDD into calendar days based on daily temperature records (p. 202–214).
3. Single Crop Coefficients (Table 6.2): Field crops load from Table 6.2. Rice differentiates seven management regimes; Jadal explicitly supports conventional continuous flooding versus water-saving intermittent irrigation (Alternate Wetting and Drying, AWD).
4. Irrigation Trigger, Refill & Cap Logic: An irrigation turn triggers when root-zone depletion Dr ≥ RAW. The event refill depth equals RAW, replenishing soil moisture without causing deep percolation. Total Available Water (TAW) acts as a hard ceiling to prevent root-zone hypoxia and fertilizer leaching.
5. Effective Rainfall: Upland crops follow the verified project engineering rule: Peff = 0.80 · (P − 3 mm) for P > 3 mm, capped at the current moisture deficit (ASSUMED project rule). Lowland flooded rice retains rainfall up to the perimeter bund spillway weir crest.

v = (1 / n) · R^(2/3) · S^(1/2)                                      [Uniform Flow Velocity, m/s]

// 2. Wetting-Front Travel Lag
lag_i = xi / (3600 · v)                                              [Travel Time from Head to Outlet i, hours]

// 4. Dry Channel Pre-Charging Fill Requirement

Section 6.0 · Multi-Agent System 2

System 2 Agents: Responsibilities & Hero Sequence

Jadal deploys seven specialized System 2 agents. Each agent possesses bounded capabilities, reads and writes system state exclusively through typed tool calls against the deterministic core, and operates under mandatory human coordinator approval for all state mutations. Arithmetic and physical balances are computed strictly by pure TypeScript functions in packages/core.

1. Orchestrator Event Dispatch

Coordinates system lifecycle events and dispatches tasks to specialized System 2 agents. Manages execution graphs and timers across Cloudflare Workflows (step.do, step.sleep). Executes zero domain arithmetic.

Trigger: System events (farmer.registered, rain.replanned, request.raised, release_window.announced, cron triggers).
Tools: Cloudflare Workflows orchestration; no direct core arithmetic tools.
Output: Routed agent execution tasks, durable workflow executions.
Human Gate: None directly; enforces approval gates on invoked target agents.
2. Intake Agent Registration

Ingests farmer registrations, plot boundaries, and seasonal crop plans across portal web forms, Telugu audio calls, and WhatsApp messages. Normalizes multi-crop plot allocations and validates cadastral records against canal outlet chainage.

Trigger: New registration submission (/api/farmers, portal form, voice intake, WhatsApp).
Tools: extract_registration, validate_plot (contract validation schemas).
Output: Structured Farmer, Plot, and CropPlan records; emits farmer.registered.
Human Gate: Coordinator verifies farmer land rights and identity (registration.verified).
3. Need Agent Agronomic Demand

Executes FAO-56 dual crop coefficient models for every registered plot. Queries Open-Meteo weather forecasts to calculate reference evapotranspiration ($ET_0$), effective rainfall, and net root-zone irrigation requirements across phenological growth stages.

Trigger: Season start, weekly planning cron, rain forecast event (rain.replanned).
Tools: crop_need, weather_forecast, propose_entitlements (gated).
Output: Proposed weekly water entitlements ($m^3$) per crop plan; emits entitlement.proposed.
Human Gate: Coordinator reviews and approves or edits entitlements (entitlement.approved).
4. Scheduler Agent Hydraulic Timetable

Section 7.0 · Transaction Lifecycle

Request State Machine & Transition Invariants

Every demand for irrigation water outside the published weekly Warabandi roster (such as acute crop distress, buffer pool requests, or post-harvest remainder transfers) travels through an explicit, auditable finite state machine. State transitions map directly to append-only domain events, guaranteeing zero unrecorded water movements:

State Machine Invariants & Double-Entry Guarantees

1. No State Mutation Without Triage: A request cannot transition directly from raised to approved. It must receive a calibrated System 1 triage score (<80ms) and a System 2 agronomic feasibility evaluation verifying soil moisture deficit ($D_r \ge \text{RAW}$) and quota availability.
2. Atomic Quota Reservation: Transitioning from recommended to approved executes an atomic D1 db.batch() transaction: the requested volume is reserved from the farmer's future seasonal quota (farmer:{id}:quota). If the request terminates in cancelled or expired, the reserved volume reverts back to the farmer's quota.
3. Physical Mass Balance Conservation: At every lifecycle stage, total canal water mass is conserved: $$\text{Season Supply} = \sum \text{Quotas} + \text{Buffer} + \sum \text{Delivered} + \text{Conveyance Losses}$$ The Auditor agent verifies this invariant continuously via the ledger_invariants tool.
4. Mandatory Human Approval Gate: System 2 agents propose recommendations via recommend_decision, but cannot commit water allocations. The human coordinator holds sole authority to execute the request.decided approval event on the console.
5. Closed-Loop Confirmation: A delivery is only marked confirmed upon explicit farmer acknowledgement via DTMF ('1'), Telugu voice confirmation, or WhatsApp reply. Unconfirmed turns trigger the coordinator escalation ladder.

Section 8.0 · Volumetric Accounting

Double-Entry Volume Ledger & Conservation Invariant

The Warabandi hours register records time rather than water volume. Because transit seepage, channel filling, and upstream overruns reduce tail-end flow rate, equal clock hours deliver unequal water. Jadal replaces the hours register with an immutable, double-entry volumetric ledger denominated in cubic metres (m³). Every drop released from the reservoir headworks is tracked through debits and credits across five typed accounts governed by the conservation of mass:

Mass Balance Conservation Invariant (packages/contracts/src/core.ts) 
season_supply ≡ Σ quotas + buffer + Σ delivered + conveyance_losses

Where:
• season_supply     = Total volume authorized and released into canal headworks (m³)
• Σ quotas          = Sum of active, unfulfilled farmer entitlements remaining in crop plans (m³)
• buffer            = Common liquidity pool: rain savings, early harvest surrender, skipped turns (m³)
• Σ delivered       = Physical water confirmed delivered at turnout field gates with verified ACK (m³)
• conveyance_losses = Physical seepage loss Q_0 · (1 - e^-kx) plus initial channel wetting volume (m³)

Invariant Check: checkConservation(entries, seasonSupply_m3, tolerance_m3) must satisfy diff_m3 ≤ tolerance_m3 (default 0 m³).
Atomicity Rule:  Every double-entry transaction executes inside a Cloudflare D1 db.batch() block (ADR-001).
